Seven ways to frame a window.

Rethink the little preview above a task tile: calmer chrome, clearer identity, and a thumbnail that feels intentionally framed. Seven distinct silhouettes, all grounded in JasonShell’s dark slate surfaces, pale-blue accents, compact geometry, and quiet elevation.

Offline concept document. No live shell actions. Thumbnails are drawn sample content, not captured windows. Close marks, task tiles, and neighboring panels are illustrations only.

Fair comparison: same editor thumbnail, window title, process name, bottom bar, and neighboring Quick Commands panel. Samples show a single-window hover preview, not the grouped-window gallery. Concept sizes are illustrative—not promises about native thumbnail layout. On narrow screens, focus each scene and use arrow keys to scroll.

01 / Quiet frame

Familiar, refined

One clean header, an app icon, and a thin thumbnail boundary. The familiar arrangement gets more breathing room without becoming a bigger panel.

Why it fits: Existing hierarchy, raised slate surface, and restrained inset highlight.

Trade-off: Polished rather than radically different.

02 / Inset lens

Recommended

A soft outer shell around a crisp recessed image. Slightly roomier padding and a subtle bevel make the preview feel finished, not like an image pasted into a tooltip.

Why it fits: Uses the shell’s small-radius, layered-control language rather than glow or heavy glass.

Trade-off: Extra padding slightly reduces image area.

03 / Caption below

Image first

Let the window arrive first. Move identity and the close mark to a calm lower caption, with a tiny pointer tying the preview to its task tile.

Why it fits: Same slate panel material; the lower caption visually relates to the bar beneath it.

Trade-off: Close moves from its familiar upper-right location.

04 / Side rail

Workbench character

A narrow utility spine holds app identity, accent, and close. The title and image sit beside it—a little more architectural, still recognizably shell chrome.

Why it fits: Echoes side rails in editor and browser panels; pale-blue spine relates to the active task cue.

Trade-off: Wider footprint, less conventional close placement.

05 / Precision edge

Sharp and minimal

No nested frame. A single pale-blue left edge and a fine perimeter hold a flush image beneath a lean header. More instrument panel than soft card.

Why it fits: Repeats the active task’s left marker and the shell’s crisp divider system.

Trade-off: Less soft; stronger edge competes slightly with thumbnail detail.

06 / Window ledger

Low-profile alternative

A compact landscape strip: thumbnail left, identity right. It occupies less vertical desktop space and makes the window name the main reading target.

Why it fits: Reads like a compact shell list item, with the same sunken-image and raised-panel contrast.

Trade-off: Thumbnail detail is harder to recognize at this size.

07 / Tabbed canopy

Architectural silhouette

A short title tab rises above the image frame. The offset silhouette separates identity from the thumbnail, borrowing the shell’s shallow-tab language rather than adding decoration.

Why it fits: Small corners and shallow tab geometry stay consistent with task and editor surfaces.

Trade-off: Shorter title area truncates long window names sooner.

Start with 02. Compare with 03 and 05.

Inset lens is the safest visual upgrade: clean, intentional, and comfortably in-family. Choose Caption below for image-first simplicity, or Precision edge for a sharper workbench. Side rail and Tabbed canopy are the more characterful options; Window ledger prioritizes compact height.
